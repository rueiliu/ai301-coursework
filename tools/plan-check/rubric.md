# Rubric: is this plan ready to post and build from?

## Checks

| Check | Evidence | Pass condition | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| `diagnosis-grounded` | The plan's stated cause, read against every step and control run in the repro evidence. | The stated cause is consistent with every observation in the repro evidence — no control run, side-by-side comparison, or step in the evidence contradicts it. When the evidence includes a control that isolates a different variable (a flag removed, a path substituted, an alternate context tried), the diagnosis must survive that control, not just the headline symptom. A diagnosis that explains the symptom but is ruled out by one of the evidence's own controls fails, however confidently it is argued. | required |
| `scope-bounded` | The plan's stated scope (in-scope / not-in-scope lines, the files or sites named) read against the repro evidence and the issue. | The plan describes one change at the site(s) the repro evidence and issue actually implicate, and explicitly excludes related-but-separate work. It fails when the plan bundles the isolated fix together with unrelated rework the issue never asked for — a migration, a redesign, a new abstraction layer, a CI matrix, a feature — even when that work is well-reasoned on its own. A plan that names a narrower fix than the full defect class and says why (an explicit, reasoned deferral) passes; a plan that expands past the defect into adjacent improvement fails. | required |
| `executable` | The plan's approach and files/areas, read as a stranger with no access to the author who must start work from the plan alone. | The plan names the specific file(s) or location(s) to change and commits to one chosen approach for the core fix. It fails when a real decision about the core fix is left open for build time ("upstream or vendored, whichever is easier", "wherever the input stack turns out to live", "profile and see"), when no file or area is named at all, or when the next action is "investigate" rather than a change. Open questions about a genuinely secondary detail (which of two already-equivalent call sites to also audit) do not fail this check; an open question about the fix itself does. | required |
| `test-plan-decisive` | The plan's test plan, read against the repro evidence's exact steps and artifacts. | The test plan names a specific, observable result tied to the repro's own command, input, or artifact (an exit code, a printed value, a specific string, a named control re-run unchanged) that a reader could check and agree was met or not. It fails on a generic standard that names no outcome specific to this fix: "run the full test suite", "make sure nothing regresses", "should feel fast/better", "nothing else should feel broken". Re-running the exact repro steps and stating the expected result passes even when it is the only test named. | required |
| `comms-conventions` | The plan comment, read against the issue's thread highlights (or the live thread) for any maintainer direction already settled there, and against the repo-facts block's stated contribution and AI-use policy. | Two things, both required. (1) Thread: when a maintainer has already proposed, confirmed, or pointed at a specific cause or fix location in the thread, the plan comment engages that direction — builds on it, or states plainly why it takes a different one — rather than silently proposing an unrelated approach as if the thread said nothing. Silence in the thread (no maintainer direction yet) imposes no such duty. (2) Policy: if the repo's stated policy requires disclosing AI assistance, the comment discloses it, naming what the assistance was; a policy that only asks for understanding, testing, or human-written comments, or that says nothing, imposes no disclosure duty and the comment passes without one. | required |
| `risks-named` | The plan's stated risks, unknowns, and deferred items, read against the confidence of its claims elsewhere. | Genuine unknowns are named as unknowns rather than folded silently into the plan as settled. A plan that defers related work states why it is deferring rather than simply omitting it. | preferred |
| `prior-art-engaged` | The issue's thread highlights and any linked PRs or prior attempts, read against the plan's approach. | The plan acknowledges an existing linked PR or a prior attempt at the same fix, and says how it relates (builds on it, supersedes it, cross-references it) rather than proceeding as if it were the first attempt. | preferred |

## Verdict rule

Accept (ready to post and build from) only if every `required` check passes.
A single `required` fail holds the package. `unclear` on a `required` check
counts as a fail: a plan I cannot verify against the evidence in the package
is not a plan that is ready to build from. `preferred` checks never change
the verdict; they distinguish a plan that is merely ready from one that is
easy to act on and unlikely to need a follow-up correction mid-build.
